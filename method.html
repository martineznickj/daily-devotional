The Method

How we learn and teach

Everything on this site runs through one method. Seven moves. Each is an old skill with a new name and one simple question.

The Seven Moves
  • Gather · Grammar · humility

    Take in what is actually there.

    The words, the terms, the raw material. Before you decide what anything means, you gather what it says.

    The question: what does it actually say?
  • Filter · Logic · discernment

    Separate signal from noise.

    Decide what a thing means, and what it doesn't. Keep the true. Let the rest fall away.

    The question: what does it mean, and what doesn't it?
  • Deliver · Rhetoric · generosity

    Say it in your own words.

    Put the idea into language that lands on another person. If you can't say it plainly, you haven't gathered it yet.

    The question: how would I say this so it lands?
  • Count · Arithmetic · honesty

    Attach the numbers.

    What is big, what is small, what changes, what repeats. Numbers don't flatter, and that's the point.

    The question: what do the numbers and the structure tell me?
  • Shape · Geometry · clarity

    See how the parts fit.

    One piece makes sense only inside the whole. Find where it sits, and the outline appears.

    The question: how does this piece fit the whole?
  • Flow · Music · patience

    Catch the rhythm.

    Timing, pattern, the thing that repeats. Notice what comes around again, and when.

    The question: what rhythm or pattern repeats?
  • Zoom · Astronomy · perspective

    Step back and see the whole system.

    Climb out of the detail. From the height, the parts you gathered form one moving picture.

    The question: what does this look like from the whole story's height?
The Pipeline

They run in order: Gather, Filter, Deliver, Count, Shape, Flow, Zoom. But it's a loop, not a ladder. You return to Gather constantly, and every later move sends you back to check.

Each move carries a virtue. Gathering is humility. Filtering is discernment. Delivering is generosity. Counting is honesty. Shaping is clarity. Flow is patience. Zoom is perspective. The skill and the character grow together.

The Daily Rhythm

Each day's devotional uses one move as its leading question, so the week itself is the method.

Gather. Read the passage slowly and name what it actually says.
Tue
Filter. What does it mean, and what doesn't it?
Wed
Deliver. Put the idea in your own words, one sentence.
Thu
Count. The numbers, the structure, the repetitions.
Fri
Shape. How this piece fits the whole story.
Sat
Flow. What rhythm or pattern repeats.
Sun
Zoom. Step back and review the week from the height.
Where It Comes From

These seven moves are the classical liberal arts, renamed. The three language arts are Grammar, Logic, and Rhetoric. The four number arts are Arithmetic, Geometry, Music, and Astronomy. Schools taught them for two thousand years, then mostly stopped. We still use them, just with plain names.

We test everything against the text with these moves. If an idea can't survive Gather and Filter, it doesn't go in.
